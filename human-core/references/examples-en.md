# English: Before/After Examples

## Example 1: Throat-Clearing + Binary Contrast

**Before:**
> "Here's the thing: building products is hard. Not because the technology is complex. Because people are complex. Let that sink in."

**After:**
> "Building products is hard. Technology is manageable. People aren't."

Removed opener, binary contrast, emphasis crutch.

---

## Example 2: Filler + Unnecessary Reassurance

**Before:**
> "It turns out that most teams struggle with alignment. The uncomfortable truth is that nobody wants to admit they're confused. And that's okay."

**After:**
> "Teams struggle with alignment. Nobody admits confusion."

Cut hedging ("most"), throat-clearing phrases, permission-granting ending.

---

## Example 3: Business Jargon Stack

**Before:**
> "In today's fast-paced landscape, we need to lean into discomfort and navigate uncertainty with clarity. This matters because your competition isn't waiting."

**After:**
> "Move faster. Your competition is."

Core message in six words.

---

## Example 4: Dramatic Fragmentation

**Before:**
> "Speed. Quality. Cost. You can only pick two. That's it. That's the tradeoff."

**After:**
> "Speed, quality, cost — pick two."

Single sentence. No performative emphasis.

---

## Example 5: Rhetorical Setup

**Before:**
> "What if I told you that the best teams don't optimize for productivity? Here's what I mean: they optimize for learning. Think about it."

**After:**
> "The best teams optimize for learning, not productivity."

Direct claim. No scaffolding.

---

## Example 6: AI Vocabulary Cluster

**Before:**
> "The new software update serves as a testament to the company's unwavering commitment to innovation. Moreover, it delivers a seamless, intuitive, and robust user experience — ensuring that users can navigate their goals with unprecedented efficiency. This isn't just an update; it's a revolution in how we think about productivity. Industry experts believe this will have a lasting impact on the evolving technological landscape, underscoring the company's pivotal role in shaping the future."

**After:**
> "The update added batch processing, keyboard shortcuts, and offline mode. Early testers finished tasks faster. Whether that holds at scale is another question."

Changes: removed "testament" (AI vocab), "moreover" (filler), "seamless, intuitive, and robust" (three-item list + promo), em dash + "-ing phrase", "not just X, it's Y" (negative listing), "industry experts believe" (vague attribution), "pivotal role" + "evolving landscape" (AI vocab). Added specifics and honest hedge.

---

## Example 7: Promotional Language

**Before:**
> "Nestled in the breathtaking region of Gondar, Ethiopia, Alamata Raya Kobo is a vibrant town boasting a rich cultural heritage and captivating natural beauty."

**After:**
> "Alamata Raya Kobo is a town in the Gondar region. It's known for its weekly market and an 18th-century church."

Removed: "nestled" "breathtaking" "vibrant" "boasting" "rich" "captivating" (promotional vocabulary). Added specifics.

---

## Example 8: False Agency + Passive Voice

**Before:**
> "The complaint becomes a fix. The data tells us what matters. As the decision emerges, the culture shifts toward accountability."

**After:**
> "An engineer fixed the bug that afternoon. We looked at the support tickets and picked the three worst. The VP told the team: from now on, you own your incidents."

Named the humans. Gave specifics.

---

## Example 9: Staccato Trio + LinkedIn Sass

**Before:**
> "No theory. No fluff. Just execution. Here's the thing: most founders don't fail because of bad ideas. They fail because they overthink. And honestly? That's the real unlock."

**After:**
> "Most founders I've worked with didn't fail from bad ideas. They stalled because they spent months refining a pitch deck nobody asked for."

Removed template drama, false conflict, manufactured insight. Added specific observation.

---

## Example 10: Formulaic Challenge Section

**Before:**
> "Despite its impressive growth, the company faces several challenges, including increased competition and regulatory uncertainty. Nevertheless, with its commitment to innovation and strategic vision, the future looks bright. The company is well-positioned to capitalize on emerging opportunities in the evolving technological landscape."

**After:**
> "Two competitors launched similar products in Q3. The EU's proposed data rules could require a full rewrite of the analytics pipeline. The company hasn't said how it plans to handle either."

Named the challenges. Dropped the forced optimism.

---

## Example 11: Present-Participle Appendage

**Before:**
> "The temple's blue, green, and gold hues resonate with the region's natural beauty, symbolizing Texas bluebonnets, the Gulf of Mexico, and the diverse Texas landscape, reflecting the community's deep connection to the land."

**After:**
> "The temple uses blue, green, and gold. The architect said the colors echo local bluebonnets and the Gulf coast."

Cut the "-ing" chain. Named the source. Let the fact stand alone.

---

## Example 12: Copulatives Avoidance

**Before:**
> "Gallery 825 serves as LAAA's contemporary art exhibition space. The gallery features four distinct spaces, boasting over 3,000 square feet."

**After:**
> "Gallery 825 is LAAA's exhibition space. It has four rooms, about 3,000 square feet total."

Used "is" and "has" instead of "serves as" and "boasts."
